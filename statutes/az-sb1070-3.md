# S.B. 1070 § 3 — state crime for failing to carry registration documents

## Excerpt

> It is a class 1 misdemeanor for a person who is unlawfully present in the
> United States to willfully fail to complete or carry an alien registration
> document, if the federal registration requirements apply to that person.

## Formalization notes — now caught by field preemption

This provision and the federal one are **both** `prohibited`. They agree on
the substance, so there is no deontic contradiction here and there never
was — for a long time this pair was the corpus's documented false negative.

The Supreme Court nonetheless struck § 3 down, on **field preemption**:
Congress's registration scheme is so comprehensive that it leaves no room
for *any* state law on the subject, including one that merely duplicates it.
Arizona also allowed no probation or pardon, so its penalty could exceed the
federal one for identical conduct.

That is a different logical shape from allowed-vs-prohibited, and the tool
now models it. [`us-ina-alien-registration`](us-ina-alien-registration.md)
carries `exclusive: true`, and this rule is displaced by it regardless of
what it says — which is the whole point of field preemption, and why the
generated Lean theorem uses `field_preemption_excl` rather than either
deontic exclusion axiom.

## Status

**Struck down.** *Arizona v. United States*, 567 U.S. 387, 400-403 (2012).
