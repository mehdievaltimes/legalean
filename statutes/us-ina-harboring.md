# INA § 274(a) — federal harboring and transporting offenses

## Excerpt

> Any person who, knowing or in reckless disregard of the fact that an alien
> has come to, entered, or remains in the United States in violation of law,
> transports or moves such alien within the United States in furtherance of
> that violation, or conceals, harbors, or shields such alien from
> detection, commits a federal felony. It is not a violation for a religious
> denomination, or an agent or officer thereof, to encourage, invite, call,
> allow, or enable an alien who is present in the United States to perform
> the vocation of a minister or missionary for the denomination.

## Formalization notes — the exemption is dropped, and it matters

`action: prohibited` with `condition: always` is a lossy reading. The second
sentence of the excerpt is the § 1324(a)(1)(C) safe harbor for certain
religious activities, and conduct inside that safe harbor is **not**
federally prohibited.

That dropped exemption is precisely where the real conflict with
[`az-sb1070-13-2929`](az-sb1070-13-2929.md) lives — Arizona's statute has no
equivalent carve-out, so it reaches conduct Congress chose to protect. The
tool therefore reports nothing about a pair that a court found conflict
preempted. See that file for the full explanation; it is the corpus's
sharpest illustration that formalization fidelity, not the Lean layer, is
the weak link.
