# S.B. 1070 § 5(A) — state harboring and transporting offense

## Excerpt

> It is unlawful for a person who is in violation of a criminal offense to
> transport or move an alien in this state, or to conceal, harbor, or shield
> an alien from detection, knowing or in reckless disregard of the fact that
> the alien has come to, entered, or remains in the United States in
> violation of law.

## Formalization notes — the second deliberate false negative

Like the registration pair, both sides are `prohibited`, so this tool finds
no contradiction. The Ninth Circuit nonetheless held the provision
preempted, again on **field/obstacle** grounds: Congress's harboring scheme
is comprehensive, and a parallel state offense interferes with the federal
government's control over enforcement priorities.

Two independent instances of the same blind spot in a twelve-excerpt corpus
is a fair indication of how common this shape is in immigration preemption
— and of how narrow the deontic-contradiction check really is.

## Status

**Struck down.** *Valle del Sol Inc. v. Whiting*, 732 F.3d 1006 (9th Cir.
2013). This provision was not among the four at issue in *Arizona v. United
States*, which is why it was litigated separately.
