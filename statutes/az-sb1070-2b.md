# S.B. 1070 § 2(B) — the "show me your papers" provision

## Excerpt

> For any lawful stop, detention, or arrest made by a state or local law
> enforcement officer, where reasonable suspicion exists that the person is
> an alien and is unlawfully present in the United States, a reasonable
> attempt shall be made, when practicable, to determine the immigration
> status of the person, including verification with the federal government.

## Formalization notes

`required` — the provision commands the attempt ("shall be made"). Paired
with the federal `allowed`, this is compatible, which is the right answer:
the Court declined to enjoin § 2(B) on its face.

The formalization drops § 2(B)'s own qualifiers ("reasonable suspicion",
"when practicable"), which the single-predicate `condition` field cannot
express. That is a real loss of fidelity, and part of why this tool reports
structure rather than legal conclusions.

## Status

**Upheld on its face** (as-applied challenges left open). *Arizona v. United
States*, 567 U.S. 387, 411-415 (2012): it was improper to enjoin the
provision before state courts had construed it and without evidence of how
it would actually operate.
