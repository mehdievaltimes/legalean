# H.B. 56 § 10 — Alabama's alien-registration offense

## Excerpt

> It is a state crime for an unlawfully present alien to willfully fail to
> complete or carry an alien registration document. An unlawfully present
> alien violates this section when he or she is found to be in violation of
> 8 U.S.C. § 1304(e) or 8 U.S.C. § 1306(a), the federal provisions governing
> alien registration. A violation carries a fine of up to $100 and not more
> than thirty days in prison.

## Formalization notes — preemption fan-out

Like [`az-sb1070-3`](az-sb1070-3.md), this rule is `prohibited` and so is
the federal one, which means there is **no deontic contradiction here
either**. It is caught only because
[`us-ina-alien-registration`](us-ina-alien-registration.md) carries
`exclusive: true`.

That makes this the preemption counterpart to
[`al-hb56-11a`](al-hb56-11a.md)'s fan-out: a single `exclusive` federal rule
now displaces parallel state offenses in **two different states**, from one
flag, with no extra configuration. Alabama and Arizona are never compared to
each other — their scopes do not overlap, and neither claims exclusivity.

Note also how tightly this provision tracks federal law: it is *defined* by
reference to violations of §§ 1304(e) and 1306(a). It could hardly agree
with federal law more completely, and it is preempted anyway. That is the
whole content of field preemption, and why the tool needs an axiom that
ignores what the displaced rule says.

## Status

**Permanently enjoined.** The Eleventh Circuit held § 10 preempted: it
"intrudes into the field of alien registration, which the Supreme Court has
confirmed is an area of exclusive federal concern," and because federal law
occupies that field, even complementary state regulation is impermissible.
As with § 11(a), Alabama conceded the point in supplemental briefing after
*Arizona*. The district court permanently enjoined §§ 10, 11(a), 13, 16, 17
and 27 by order of November 25, 2013.
