# IRCA — no federal criminal penalty on the unauthorized worker

## Excerpt

> The Immigration Reform and Control Act imposes civil fines and, for
> repeated violations, criminal penalties on employers who knowingly hire
> unauthorized workers. Congress considered and rejected imposing federal
> criminal penalties on an unauthorized alien for the act of merely seeking
> or engaging in unauthorized employment; that conduct carries no federal
> criminal sanction.

## Formalization notes

`action: allowed` here means **not criminally prohibited**, which is a
simplification worth stating plainly: unauthorized employment still carries
civil immigration consequences for the worker. What the formalization
captures is Congress's deliberate choice to leave the worker's own conduct
outside the criminal law — the same choice the Supreme Court relied on when
it struck down S.B. 1070 § 5(C).

## Status

Good law. The Court described this congressional choice at length in
*Arizona v. United States*, 567 U.S. 387, 403-407 (2012), reasoning that
Congress had considered criminalizing the unauthorized worker's own conduct
and declined to do so.

## Conflicts with

- [`az-sb1070-5c`](az-sb1070-5c.md) — Arizona criminalized exactly this conduct.
