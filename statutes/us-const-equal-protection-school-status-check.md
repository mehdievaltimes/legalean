# Equal protection — states may not gate school enrollment on status disclosure

## Excerpt

> A state law that requires public elementary and secondary schools to
> determine, upon enrollment, whether a child was born outside the United
> States or is the child of an alien not lawfully present substantially
> burdens the right secured by *Plyler v. Doe*. Such a provision may be
> upheld only if it furthers some substantial state interest. State-mandated
> disclosure of a child's immigration status upon enrollment erects a
> barrier to the free public education *Plyler* protects, and no substantial
> state interest justifies that interference.

## Formalization notes

`prohibited` for the school district: it may not condition enrollment on
determining and recording immigration status.

Note this is a **second, distinct rule drawn from the same constitutional
clause** as [`us-const-equal-protection-education`](us-const-equal-protection-education.md).
They are separate rules with separate `activity` values — denying education
outright is not the same act as demanding status at the schoolhouse door —
and the tool correctly never compares them to each other.

Same caveat as the other constitutional rule: the real doctrine is
heightened scrutiny with a "substantial state interest" escape hatch, and
flattening it to an unconditional `prohibited` overstates it. Here the
Eleventh Circuit noted Alabama had only ever defended § 28 under rational
basis, which alone doomed it.

## Status

Good law. *Plyler*'s protection remains controlling as of 2026; the
Eleventh Circuit's application of it to enrollment status checks was never
disturbed, and Alabama's provision was ultimately enjoined permanently.
