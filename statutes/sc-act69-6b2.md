# S.C. Act 69 § 6(B)(2) — false identification to prove lawful presence

## Excerpt

> It is unlawful for a person to display, cause or permit to be displayed, or
> have in their possession a false, fictitious, fraudulent, or counterfeit
> picture identification with the intent to prove that they are lawfully
> present in the United States.

## Formalization notes

Keyed as a **specialization** of the federal field, the same treatment given
[`ga-hb87-7`](ga-hb87-7.md) and [`sc-act69-4bd`](sc-act69-4bd.md): the offense
is narrower than the federal scheme because it reaches only documents used to
prove *lawful presence*, so the key names that narrower act and
`_within_field` does the matching. Field preemption fires; no contradiction is
claimed, and none is available anyway since both sides say `prohibited`.

This is South Carolina's second entry in the corpus and its second occupied
field — § 4(B),(D) sits inside the harboring field, this one inside document
fraud. A single state statute can intrude on more than one federal scheme,
and the tool tracks them separately because the fields are declared by
different federal rules.

## Status

**Preliminarily enjoined.** *United States v. South Carolina*, 720 F.3d 518
(4th Cir. 2013) held § 6(B)(2) **both** field and conflict preempted:
Congress has occupied the field of fraudulent immigration documents through
8 U.S.C. § 1324c and 18 U.S.C. § 1546, and the state provision separately
conflicts with the prosecutorial discretion those schemes vest in federal
officials.

As with § 4(B),(D), the citation carries no S.C. Code section because the
opinion identifies the provision only as a section of Act 69.
