# H.B. 56 § 11(a) — Alabama's version of the unauthorized-work offense

## Excerpt

> It is unlawful for a person who is an unauthorized alien to knowingly
> apply for work, solicit work in a public or private place, or perform work
> as an employee or independent contractor in this state. An alien who is
> authorized to work in the United States is not subject to penalty under
> this section, and the section is otherwise to be construed as consistent
> with 8 U.S.C. § 1324a.

## Formalization notes — the fan-out case

This is deliberately the *same* `subject` and `activity` as
[`az-sb1070-5c`](az-sb1070-5c.md), because the two states enacted
substantively the same offense. It exists in the corpus to demonstrate a
property none of the other pairs do: **one federal rule checked against
every state in the corpus at once**. The single federal rule in
[`us-ina-employment-noncriminalization`](us-ina-employment-noncriminalization.md)
now yields two independently verified conflicts, one per state, with no
extra configuration.

It also exercises the negative half of the scope table. Alabama and Arizona
both prohibit this conduct, but `scopes.py` treats two states as
**non-overlapping**, so the tool never compares them to each other — which
is right twice over: their actions agree anyway, and one state's law has no
force in the other.

The Eleventh Circuit made the same point in legal terms: § 11(a) "cannot be
meaningfully distinguished" from Arizona's § 5(C).

## Status

**Permanently enjoined.** The Eleventh Circuit held § 11(a) preempted,
following *Arizona v. United States* directly — and noted that in
supplemental briefing after *Arizona*, Alabama itself conceded the point.
The district court permanently enjoined §§ 10, 11(a), 13, 16, 17 and 27 of
H.B. 56 by order of November 25, 2013.
