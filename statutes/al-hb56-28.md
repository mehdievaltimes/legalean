# H.B. 56 § 28 — mandatory enrollment status determination

## Excerpt

> Every public elementary and secondary school in this state shall
> determine, at the time of enrollment, whether an enrolling child was born
> outside the jurisdiction of the United States or is the child of an alien
> not lawfully present in the United States. The determination shall be made
> on the basis of the child's birth certificate. If no birth certificate is
> available, or if the certificate reflects that the student was born
> outside the United States or is the child of an alien not lawfully present
> in the United States, the parent or guardian shall notify the school of
> the actual citizenship or immigration status of the student.

## Formalization notes — the first `required` half of a conflict

This is the corpus's first conflict turning on **`prohibited` vs
`required`** rather than `allowed` vs `prohibited`, so its generated proof
closes with `prohibited_required_excl` instead of
`allowed_prohibited_excl`. Until this pair, that second axiom in
[`lean/Legalean/Deontic.lean`](../lean/Legalean/Deontic.lean) was exercised
only by a smoke test, never by real law.

It is also the pair that shows why `allowed`/`required` is *not* axiomatized
as exclusive while `prohibited`/`required` is: a state may freely require
what federal law merely permits (the E-Verify and § 2(B) pairs), but it
cannot require what federal law forbids.

## Status

**Permanently enjoined.** The Eleventh Circuit held the plaintiffs likely to
succeed on their claim that § 28 violates the Equal Protection Clause,
reversed, and remanded for entry of a preliminary injunction, 691 F.3d 1236
(11th Cir. Aug. 20, 2012) (Nos. 11-14535, 11-14675). Under a settlement
approved November 25, 2013, Alabama agreed not to enforce the enrollment
verification requirement and § 28 was permanently enjoined.
